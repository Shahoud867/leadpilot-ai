01

Startup idea: LeadPilot AI

The problem and the proposed solution.

The problem

Interested customers can get forgotten.

A course inquiry arrives on WhatsApp. Another arrives on Instagram. Staff use different chats or spreadsheets, so a reply is late, two people handle the same person, or nobody follows up.

The solution

One list. A named owner. A next step.

Staff enter inquiries or import a spreadsheet. LeadPilot checks for repeated entries, suggests who should handle each inquiry, sets a reply time, and tells the manager when something is missed.

What is a “lead”? Simply a person who has asked about a course, a property, or another service.

How the helpers work together

The MAS / game diagram. MAS means several small software helpers working as a team. Each helper is called an agent.

03

Who would use it?

Small businesses with a steady flow of inquiries and around 3–20 people handling sales or admissions.

First customer group

Training and education institutes

For example, an IELTS or IT training institute. Staff need to answer course questions, arrange visits, and follow up with people interested in joining.

Second customer group

Small property businesses

For example, a team handling questions about plots or apartments. Staff need clear ownership of each inquiry and reminders for calls or visits.

Who pays? Usually the owner, branch head, or sales manager.

Who uses it daily? Admissions advisers, sales staff, and their manager.

05

Where does game theory fit?

It helps explain how one person’s choices affect the rest of the team.

Cooperation = working toward a shared resultMatching = pairing an inquiry with a suitable person

Staff share a limited amount of time. If everyone chooses only the easiest customers, harder inquiries may be ignored. If every new inquiry goes to one busy adviser, replies may slow down. LeadPilot uses a shared assignment rule to give each inquiry a clear owner and spread the work more fairly.

A simple example — fictional advisers, not real performance data

Adviser B

Suggested choice: Adviser B, if B is working and can answer in time. B has more room for the new inquiry.

The shared goal: help the team answer more inquiries on time. The manager can change the choice and record why. The demo applies the rule; its effect on real staff behavior and sales still needs a customer trial.

06

Why would a business pay for it?

To make follow-up easier to manage and reduce the chance of forgetting an interested customer.

  • ✓
    A clear owner for every inquiry

    Staff and managers know who should take the next step.

  • ✓
    Visible reminders and missed replies

    The manager can act before an inquiry is forgotten.

  • ✓
    Less manual checking

    One shared list makes it easier to see what still needs attention.

  • ✓
    Reasons behind each suggestion

    The manager can see why a teammate was suggested and change the decision.

The proposed value is better day-to-day control of customer inquiries. More sales or higher profit would need to be measured in a real business trial.
